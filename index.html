The Six As-Ifs

A 1,500-year-old argument, in six objects

The Diamond Sūtra (金剛經) closes with a four-line verse. It doesn't argue that everything is impermanent — it just names six things you already know don't last, and asks you to look at everything conditioned the same way:

一切有為法，如夢幻泡影，如露亦如電，應作如是觀。

“All conditioned dharmas are like a dream, a phantasm, a bubble, a shadow — like dew, or like lightning. Contemplate them thus.”

Six similes, each doing different work: a dream fools you from the inside; an illusion fools you from the outside; a bubble looks solid but is only ever a surface; a shadow was never a thing at all; dew is real but keeps a schedule you don't control; lightning is over before you can even name it. Below are six small machines built to make that argument the way the verse does — not by telling you, but by handing you something and watching it not stay held.

Each one is a button (or two) — try it with a mouse, or tab to it and use the keyboard. Nothing below is a trick to catch you out; every one plays fair and still won't stay still.

Six images, one insight: each shows a different way things cannot be firmly held.

夢 · A dream

A dream feels solid while you're in it. Try to pin one word down.

It feels real while you are in it, yet it cannot be held onto.

幻 · An illusion

A conjuror's shape looks like it has weight and a far side. Turn it around.

a solid thing
(nothing behind it)

It appears real, but there is no solid thing behind it.

泡 · A bubble

Hold the button to blow it up. Being careful doesn't change how this ends.

It arises, but it is fragile and bursts easily.

影 · A shadow

Move the light with the arrow keys (or drag it). Then try to grab the shadow itself.

It can be seen, but it depends on conditions and cannot be grasped.

Focus the stage above, then use the arrow keys to move the light.

露 · Dew

This one is honest about its clock. Watch it form, then try to stop it from going.

It exists only briefly, then quickly disappears.

電 · Lightning

Strike it, then replay in slow motion. Slow motion doesn't buy you a middle.

It flashes into being and vanishes in an instant.

Nothing above was rigged

Each station played by its own rules and still ended the same way. That repetition is the verse's method, not a coincidence of this build: it doesn't ask you to accept impermanence on authority, it keeps handing you something to hold until you notice your hands were never going to be enough.

The verse's last line isn't just a description — 應作如是觀, “contemplate them thus,” is an instruction. Every station above also has a quieter button: Let it be. It doesn't stop anything from happening, and it earns nothing you can point to — only its own tally, kept separately from the one for holding on. That second number is the part of the argument this page can't make by showing you failure alone.

Translation above is my own plain-English rendering, checked against Charles Muller's and Red Pine's published English translations of the Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra rather than invented; scholars differ on exactly how to render 如 (“as”, or “suchness”) into English, and this page picks one reading among several defensible ones.
